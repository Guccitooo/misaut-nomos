## 1. Corporate chain: which entity holds the Herbex stake?

### Takeaway
The notifying party in EU case M.12447 is **Flamingo Produce Investments** (UK, Flamingo group), which takes joint control of Herbex Iberia together with **Fondo Nazca IV**. The ultimate controller is **Sun Capital** (USA). The UK holding chain, as far as public records show, runs Zara UK Holdco Ltd → Zara UK Topco Ltd → Flamingo Group International Ltd → operating subsidiaries. I could not confirm the company number of Flamingo Produce Investments or where exactly it sits under FGI.

### Cited Findings
- EU case M.12447: on 23 July 2026 the Commission received notification of a concentration in which **Flamingo Produce Investments** (UK, part of the Flamingo Group, "ultimately indirectly controlled by Sun Capital", USA) and **Fondo Nazca IV** (Spain) acquire joint control of Herbex. It was published in the OJ as "FLAMINGO / FONDO NAZCA IV / HERBEX" — [EUR-Lex OJ C/2026/4235](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC_202604235); [IEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-clears-acquisition-of-herbex-by-flamingo-and-nazca/1247729)

The target is described as **Herbex Iberia and its subsidiaries**, with "negligible activities in the EEA", and the parties do not operate in the same or connected markets — [IEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-clears-acquisition-of-herbex-by-flamingo-and-nazca/1247729); [Brussels Times (snippet)](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2276093/eu-clears-uk-spanish-control-of-fresh-herbs-venture-under-simplified-review)

## 3. Debt: term loan, maturities, lenders, ratings, covenants

### Takeaway
Flamingo Group International Ltd is the borrower of a **senior secured TLB maturing August 2028** and a **€15m RCF maturing August 2027** (undrawn at Sep 2025), plus a committed, undrawn **£22m supply-chain financing line**. Fitch upgraded the IDR to **'B' (Stable)** on 1 Dec 2025 (senior secured 'B+'/RR3) and appears to have affirmed it later. **I could not confirm the "€236m" TLB figure** or find a public S&P rating.

### Cited Findings
- Fitch, 1 Dec 2025: IDR upgraded from **'B-' to 'B', Outlook Stable**; senior secured affirmed at **'B+', RR3**. The TLB and RCF rank pari passu — [Fitch via TradingView/Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025-12-01:newsml_FITBXmFN:0-fitch-upgrades-flamingo-to-b-outlook-stable/)
- Maturities: **RCF August 2027; TLB August 2028**. The RCF is **€15m**, fully undrawn at Sep 2025. The **£22m** committed supply-chain financing line was not used at Sep 2025 — [Fitch via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025-12-01:newsml_FITBXmFN:0-fitch-upgrades-flamingo-to-b-outlook-stable/)
- Fitch sensitivities: **negative** if the EBITDA margin falls sustainably below 10% or leverage stays above 4.0x; **positive** if leverage stays below 3.0x. Rating constraints are limited scale, narrow product and geographic diversification, and exposure to a fragmented, agriculture-like floriculture market — [Fitch via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025-12-01:newsml_FITBXmFN:0-fitch-upgrades-flamingo-to-b-outlook-stable/)
- A later Fitch action, "Fitch Affirms 5 EMEA Consumer Products Companies Following Corporate Criteria Update", appears to have affirmed Flamingo at 'B'. The date was not confirmed (presumably 2026) — [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/fitch-affirms-5-emea-consumer-products-companies-following-corporate-criteria-update-ce7e5adad88ef72c)
- Distress in 2024: TCR Europe (around Sep 2024) reported that participations in FGI's syndicated loan were trading at about **83.8 cents on the dollar** on the secondary market (figure from a search snippet). It describes FGI as owned by funds managed/advised by Sun Capital Partners — [TCR Europe 26 Sep 2024](http://www.bankrupt.com/TCREUR_Public/240926.mbx)
- Original LBO debt (2017–18): to fund the merger with **Afriflora** (bought from KKR), Flamingo raised an all-leveraged loan reported at about €300m. Floral Daily reports a **€310m** leveraged loan from **Credit Suisse, Investec and Jefferies** — [Floral Daily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9013787/banks-provide-flamingo-eu310-million-leveraged-loan/); [ProQuest/LBO loans and roses](https://www.proquest.com/docview/2000956014)

### Inferences
- The August 2028 maturity points to an amend-and-extend in or around 2024, together with the equity injection (the original 2018 loan would have matured in about 2024–25). This is not confirmed. If the original was €310m and the outstanding amount today is about €236m, that would imply partial repayment as part of the 2024 restructuring. **The €236m figure is not verified**: no source I accessed gives the current TLB amount.
- From late 2026 the TLB is about 21 months from maturity, and the RCF is within 12 months of maturity from Aug 2026. Refinancing is a near-term priority, and investing in Herbex through a joint venture (rather than buying 100%) fits a sponsor that conserves cash and keeps leverage under 3.5–4x ahead of refinancing. It also fits building an "equity story" for an eventual Sun exit.
- Fitch's 4.0x threshold limits debt-financed acquisitions. A minority or joint-control stake that is not consolidated, or is equity-accounted, has less impact on leverage. Typical TLB covenants (cov-lite, with a springing covenant on the RCF and permitted-investment baskets) are **likely but not verified**.

## 5. Acquisition history and post-deal integration

### Takeaway
Flamingo has grown through **100% acquisitions with full integration**: Afriflora (Ethiopia, 2017–18, bought from KKR), Bigot group (France, Dec 2021) and Bigot Flower Kenya (Jun 2023). Herbex is the first known case of **shared (joint) control** with a financial partner.

### Cited Findings
- **Afriflora** (Ethiopia, the world's largest exporter of roses to Europe): bought from KKR by Sun EP and merged with Flamingo in 2017–18, financed with an LBO loan of about €300–310m — [ProQuest](https://www.proquest.com/docview/2000956014); [Floral Daily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9013787/banks-provide-flamingo-eu310-million-leveraged-loan/); [AIPH interview: "We bought a business called Afriflora in Ethiopia in 2018"](https://aiph.org/floraculture/news/flamingo-horticulture-lessons-in-building-a-sustainable-scalable-and-winning-business/)
- After the deal, Afriflora kept its own brand and CEO (Bruinsma since 2024), but under the group structure — [flamingo.net/team/freark-bruinsma](https://flamingo.net/team/freark-bruinsma/)
- **Bigot group** (France, Sarthe, family-owned, 180 employees): acquired by Flamingo (a Sun Capital portfolio company), deal date 12 Dec 2021, advised by DLA Piper. The rationale was access to the French market, the third largest for cut flowers in Europe — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-de/news/2021/12/dla-piper-advises-flamingo); [Business Solutions Atlantic France](https://www.business-solutions-atlantic-france.com/news/plant-production-bigot-fleurs-joins-the-british-group-flamingo-horticulture/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/209575-09)
- **Bigot Flower Kenya Plc** (Naivasha): 100% of the share capital acquired by Flamingo Horticulture Investments Ltd, approved unconditionally by the Kenyan CAK in June 2023 — [Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2023/06/uk-investment-firm-flamingo-acquires-naivasha-based-flower-company/); [Floral Daily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9535568/uk-firm-acquires-full-stake-in-bigot-flower-kenya/)
- Earlier history: Flamingo Holdings (Kenya/UK) was the former Finlays Horticulture. CDC (now BII) took a 14% stake in 2003 — [Actis](https://www.act.is/2003/05/22/cdc-buys-a-14-stake-in-flamingo-holdings-a-fully-integrated-horticultural-business/)

### Inferences
- Pattern: Flamingo buys **100%** and imposes group control (finance, sales to UK retailers, compliance), even when it keeps local brands and management. Joint control of Herbex with Nazca IV is a **break with that pattern**. It could be a first step toward a majority stake, given that Nazca has held Herbex since 2018 and a fund exit is due by fund life, but no evidence of call options was found.

### Gaps
- Price and structure of each deal; integration evidence (management changes, synergies) at Bigot France.
- Other deals 2019–2026 (vegetables, prepared produce).

## 6. Customers and categories; Morocco/Spain sourcing before Herbex

### Takeaway
Flamingo is a key supplier of flowers and premium vegetables to **Tesco** (its anchor customer), M&S, Sainsbury's and Waitrose. **Herbex was already in Flamingo Produce's supply chain before the deal**: Tesco's 2024 supplier list includes a site called **"Herbex North" (Nador, Morocco) under Flamingo Produce**, alongside Flamingo Produce sites in Agadir.

### Cited Findings
- Tesco 2024 Primary Supplier Disclosure List: under **Flamingo Produce** it lists Kenyan packhouses (Nanyuki/Mount Kenya, Naivasha) and Moroccan sites in **Agadir** and **Nador**, including **"Herbex North · Flamingo Produce · Secteur Hassan, Bouarg, Nador 62000, Morocco"** — [Tesco PLC 2024 supplier list](https://www.tescoplc.com/media/asxnw5ep/2024-primary-supplier-disclosure-list-v2.pdf)
- Tesco case study: Flamingo Horticulture (Stevenage) sources produce from **Kenya, South America, Morocco and the UK**, focusing on premium and prepared speciality vegetables (green beans, Tenderstem broccoli, asparagus, carrots, baby corn) — [Tesco case study](https://www.tescoplc.com/media/755171/flamingo-case-study-final.pdf)
- Customers include Tesco, Marks & Spencer, Sainsbury's and Waitrose (directory sources). Bloomberg calls Flamingo "Tesco flower supplier", supplying flowers and vegetables to leading supermarkets — [Bloomberg 2021](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-09-30/sun-capital-said-to-weigh-sale-of-tesco-flower-supplier-flamingo); [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/flamingo-horticulture)
- Air-freight model: products reach international markets within 24h of harvest — [Fairmiles](https://fairmiles.org/news/case-study-flamingo-groups-impact-on-livelihoods-in-africa-through-airfreighted-produce/)
- Third-party products are about 65% of UK volumes (Fitch). Flamingo acts largely as a category manager or aggregator for UK retail — [Fitch via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025-12-01:newsml_FITBXmFN:0-fitch-upgrades-flamingo-to-b-outlook-stable/)
- Operations in Kenya, Tanzania, South Africa and Ethiopia, with processing/distribution in the UK, Netherlands, Germany and Kenya — [Companies-data aggregator via search](https://www.companiesintheuk.co.uk/ltd/zara-uk-topco); [Sun Capital portfolio](https://suncappart.com/portfolio/flamingo-horticulture/)

### Inferences
- Commercial leverage: Flamingo controls access to UK retail shelf space, especially at Tesco, through its role as supplier and category manager. If Herbex already supplied Tesco **through** Flamingo Produce (Herbex North under Flamingo Produce), Flamingo was a channel customer of Herbex before the deal. That gives it operational and commercial influence beyond its formal share of governance: volume, pricing, specifications and certifications for UK retailers.
- Strategic logic (inferred): Morocco and Spain give year-round, lower-cost supply of herbs to the UK by road or sea, complementing air freight from Kenya (which faces pressure on cost and carbon footprint).
- Customer concentration risk: Flamingo depends heavily on a few UK supermarkets. Tesco's weight is probably very high, but no percentage was found.

### Gaps
- Percentage of revenue from Tesco and the other main customers (in the annual report; not accessible).
- Volume or value Herbex sold through Flamingo before 2026, and whether there is an exclusive offtake agreement.
- Whether Flamingo had its own herb production in Kenya competing with Herbex Morocco.
