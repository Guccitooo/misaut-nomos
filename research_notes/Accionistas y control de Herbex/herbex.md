## 1. Corporate structure and shareholders (incl. the 2026 Flamingo/Nazca deal)

### Takeaway
The operating parent is **Herbex Iberia, S.L.** (El Ejido, Almería; CIF B04420014; incorporated 28 Jun 2002), which has subsidiaries. Until 2026 Nazca held **93.69%** and founder Jaime Visquert held the rest. In **July–August 2026** the European Commission cleared **Flamingo Produce Investments (UK; ultimately Sun Capital)** and **FONDO NAZCA IV** taking **joint control** of Herbex Iberia and its subsidiaries through a share purchase (case M.12447). Herbex is now a jointly controlled company of two financial-sponsor-backed shareholders. Joint control means each shareholder has veto rights over strategic decisions (that follows from the EU legal concept, not from disclosed terms).

### Cited Findings

The concentration is accomplished by way of purchase of shares." Examined under the simplified procedure. — [EUR-Lex OJ C notice, FLAMINGO / FONDO NAZCA IV / HERBEX](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC_202604235)

### Inferences
- Joint control under EU law means both Flamingo and Nazca hold decisive influence (vetoes on budget, business plan, major investments and senior management). Herbex's strategic autonomy is therefore structurally low, and both shareholders have strong leverage over it.
- Herbex is now a company held by two sponsors: Nazca (fund IV, invested since 2018, so about 8 years in, well past a typical holding period) and Flamingo (a Sun Capital portfolio company). Expect the shareholders to drive a defined exit horizon, an integration agenda (with Flamingo) and possibly leverage.
- The founder's residual stake (about 6.3% before the deal) may have been bought out, diluted or kept. No source confirms which.
- "Herbex Iberia S.L.U." in one directory may be stale (pre-2018) or may point to an intermediate holdco. The notice targets "Herbex Iberia, S.L. and its subsidiaries", which suggests Herbex Iberia is the top operating entity.

## 2. Financials (revenue, EBITDA, net debt, employees)

### Takeaway
Group revenue was about EUR 50–60m around 2020–2022 (more than 75% exported). Herbex Iberia's revenue fell to **EUR 46.1m in 2024** after a 2023–24 restructuring that gave up volume for margin: reported EBITDA rose from **EUR 3.1m to EUR 7.1m**. ## 4. Customers, channels and overlap with Flamingo

### Takeaway
Herbex sells under the Herbex and Bio Herbex brands to supermarkets in Spain and abroad (20–25+ countries), with more than 75% of sales exported, and it complies with Tesco-specific standards. Flamingo is a large UK produce and flowers supplier serving major UK and EU supermarkets, sourcing from Kenya, South America, Morocco and the UK, and it already sells Kenyan herbs. The EU notice says the parties do **not** operate in the same or vertically related markets, which is a regulatory-market finding and does not rule out a shared retail customer base.

### Cited Findings
- More than 75% of revenue is generated outside Spain. — [Capital-Riesgo.es](https://capital-riesgo.es/es/articles/herbex-participada-por-nazca-capital-adquiere-la-italiana-magnani/)
- Presence in more than 25 countries. — [ensun](https://ensun.io/search/herb/spain). Exports to 20 countries (older). — [La Voz de Almería](https://www.lavozdealmeria.com/economia/76321/empresa-hierbas-aromaticas-sede-ejido-exporta-veinte-paises.html)
- The group grew on the Herbex brand sold in Spanish supermarkets and abroad. It works to GlobalG.A.P., Tesco Nurture, LEAF and Field to Fork standards, which implies it supplies Tesco or Tesco's supply chain, though no explicit contract is cited. — [La Voz de Almería](https://www.lavozdealmeria.com/noticia/20/economia/230240/en-el-ejido-esta-el-lider-de-europa-de-las-hierbas-aromaticas)

### Inferences
- The strategic logic is likely complementary herb origins: Kenya (Flamingo) plus Morocco and Spain (Herbex) give year-round supply to UK and EU multiples. Flamingo probably also adds UK packing and retail account access. If Herbex routes UK volume through Flamingo's packhouses and accounts, its commercial dependence on Flamingo rises sharply, because Flamingo would control the customer relationship.
- The EC's "not same market" finding plausibly reflects narrow market definitions (fresh herbs production in the EEA), not the absence of shared customers.

### Gaps
- No named Herbex customers or customer-concentration percentages were found. No evidence was found of whether Herbex already supplies Flamingo or Flamingo's customers. Flamingo's herb volumes and origins were not found. Whether Magnani is still Herbex's Italian retail channel was not confirmed.

## 6. Risks

### Takeaway
The main risks are water scarcity in Souss-Massa (reliance on expensive desalinated water or depleted aquifers), labour-intensive harvesting, phytosanitary and MRL compliance for EU/UK retail, MAD/EUR/GBP currency exposure, retail concentration, and a history of financial stress and restructuring. Most of these are inferred from the sector, not documented for Herbex.
