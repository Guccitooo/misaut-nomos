## 1. Nazca's entry into Herbex (2018): seller, stake, valuation, advisors, management

### Takeaway
Fondo Nazca IV bought a majority stake (reported as 80%) in Herbex from its founder and CEO Jaime Visquert in October 2018. The deal included a primary capital injection for growth. No price or valuation was disclosed. Herbex then had about €47m of revenue, roughly three-quarters of it from exports. Visquert kept a significant minority and initially stayed on as CEO.

### Cited Findings
- **Date and seller (11–12 Oct 2018):** Nazca Capital acquired a majority stake in aromatic herbs specialist Herbex from the company's founder and CEO, Jaime Visquert. Visquert retained a significant minority holding and was to keep leading the business. No financial terms were disclosed. — [Unquote](https://www.unquote.com/southern-europe/official-record/3012145/nazca-capital-backs-herbex); deal date 11 Oct 2018 per [MergerLinks](https://app.mergerlinks.com/transactions/2018-10-11-herbex); also [PE Hub, Oct 2018](https://pehub.com/2018/10/nazca-capital-buys-herbex)
- **Capital injection:** Nazca's investment included a capital injection to finance Herbex's growth plan (new markets and channels). — [Unquote](https://www.unquote.com/southern-europe/official-record/3012145/nazca-capital-backs-herbex)
- **Vehicle:** Nazca used its Fondo Nazca IV (€275m). Herbex was the fund's third investment. — [Nazca press release (via capital-riesgo.es)](https://capital-riesgo.es/en/articles/herbex-acelera-su-expansi-n-internacional-e-incorpora-a-nazca-a-su-accionariado/); [Nazca.es (ES)](https://www.nazca.es/noticias/herbex-acelera-su-expansion-internacional-e-incorpora-a-nazca-a-su-accionariado/)

### Inferences
- The founder moved from controlling owner to minority partner and CEO in 2018. By 2022 he had been replaced as top executive (Rafael Martínez was CEO), and by 2024 he had left the administrative body (section 5). This is the usual pattern of a PE owner taking operational control.
- No valuation was disclosed. At typical 2018 Spanish mid-market multiples, an implied enterprise value for a €47m-revenue agri-food business could fall anywhere in the tens of millions of euros. This is speculative and should not be quoted as a figure.

## 2. Add-ons, restructuring and footprint under Nazca (Magnani, Morocco, Canaries, Senegal, "Bio Herbex")

### Takeaway
Under Nazca, Herbex bought a majority of Italy's Magnani in January 2022, financed by a capital increase from Nazca and Visquert, and revenue passed €60m. In 2023–24 Nazca then ran a "severe" restructuring: it sold the Moroccan subsidiary at Moulay-Bousalham and closed three production centres. EBITDA rose from €3.1m to €7.1m on lower sales. By June 2026 Alimarket reported Herbex hitting targets and raising profit.

### Cited Findings
- **Magnani (Jan 2022):** Herbex closed the purchase of a majority stake in Italian herb supplier Magnani, and the founding family kept day-to-day management. The deal was financed through a capital increase by the shareholders, the Nazca Capital fund and founder Jaime Visquert. — [Agrodiario, 26 Jan 2022](https://www.agrodiario.com/articulo/industria-agroalimentaria/firma-almeriense-aromaticas-frescas-herbex-compra-italiana-magnani/20220126065817036528.html); [Nazca.es](https://www.nazca.es/noticias/herbex-lider-europeo-en-hierbas-aromaticas-frescas-consolida-su-posicion-en-el-sur-de-europa-tras-la-adquisicion-de-la-italiana-magnani/); [EFA News](https://www.efanews.eu/en/item/23169-aromatic-herbs-magnani-joins-the-herbex-group.html)

## 3. Sale process (2022–2026) and the Flamingo deal (EU case M.12447)

### Takeaway
Nazca signalled in 2022 that it would exit "before or after 2024" with no fixed term. By January 2025 it had engaged Banco Sabadell to study a divestment valued at over €60m, while preferring to bring in an equity partner. Reports in April 2025 and January 2026 said Herbex had not been sold. On 23 July 2026, Flamingo Produce Investments Ltd (Flamingo Group, ultimately Sun Capital) and Fondo Nazca IV notified the acquisition of joint control of Herbex Iberia via a share purchase. The Commission cleared it under the simplified procedure on 14 August 2026 (press notice 17 August 2026). The stake split, price, rollover and shareholder-agreement terms have not been reported.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 (Efeagro/HortiDaily):** CEO Rafael Martínez did not rule out Herbex joining a larger agri-food group if Nazca, then holding 80%, exits: "it is very likely that Herbex will end up joining a larger group." Nazca said there was no fixed term for the divestment, which could happen before or after 2024. — [HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9400427/if-nazca-leaves-herbex-might-be-part-of-large-agri-food-group/); [FreshPlaza.es](https://www.freshplaza.es/spain/article/9400180/herbex-ve-su-futuro-en-un-gran-grupo-si-sale-su-principal-accionista-el-fondo-de-capital-riesgo-nazca/)
- **Jan 2025 (elEconomista, reprised by FreshPlaza/HortiDaily):**
  - Private equity funds and Herbex competitors had approached Nazca about a partnership or acquisition.
  - Nazca approached Banco Sabadell to study a possible divestment, though the priority was a new equity partner.
  - The value of such a transaction could exceed €60m.
  - Nazca was "currently not interested in selling" but open to a new investor through a partial share sale, a capital increase, or both.
  - Latest accounts showed Nazca at 93.69%, with Jaime Visquert holding the rest.
  - Sources: [elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/capital-riesgo/noticias/13178244/01/25/nazca-reestructura-herbex-y-abre-la-puerta-a-nuevos-inversores.html); [HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9697609/private-equity-funds-are-interested-in-herbex/); [FreshPlaza.es](https://www.freshplaza.es/spain/article/9697086/los-fondos-de-capital-privado-se-interesan-por-herbex/)
- **Apr 2025 (elEconomista):** Herbex, "object of desire of some competitors", would not be sold, at least in the short term. — [elEconomista, Apr 2025 (Zunibal article)](https://www.eleconomista.es/capital-riesgo/noticias/13317917/04/25/nazca-engorda-zunibal-y-da-el-salto-a-asia-con-una-compra.html)
- **Jan 2026 (elEconomista):** Nazca "tampoco ha vendido Herbex" despite expressions of interest. The firm starts 2026 with divestments pending, after an unsuccessful attempt to sell Filmin. — [elEconomista, Jan 2026](https://www.eleconomista.es/capital-riesgo/noticias/13729157/01/26/nazca-capital-remodela-su-equipo-y-nombra-nuevos-socios.html)
- **Notification (23 Jul 2026):** the Commission received notification of case M.12447 "FLAMINGO / FONDO NAZCA IV / HERBEX". Flamingo Produce Investments Ltd (UK, Flamingo Group, ultimately indirectly controlled by Sun Capital, US) and Fondo Nazca IV FCR (Spain) would acquire joint control of the whole of Herbex by purchase of shares. Before the deal, Nazca solely controlled Herbex. — [EUR-Lex OJ C/2026/04235](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC_202604235)
- **Flamingo's business:** growing, processing and supplying fresh cut flowers, house and garden plants, and premium produce. — [iEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-clears-acquisition-of-herbex-by-flamingo-and-nazca/1247729)
- **Clearance:** decision dated 14 Aug 2026, press notice 17 Aug 2026, simplified procedure. Grounds: the joint venture has negligible activities in the EEA, and the parents are not active in the same or vertically related markets. — [iEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-clears-acquisition-of-herbex-by-flamingo-and-nazca/1247729); [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2276093/eu-clears-uk-spanish-control-of-fresh-herbs-venture-under-simplified-review)

### Inferences
- **Nazca kept a meaningful stake.** The notified structure is joint control by Flamingo and Fondo Nazca IV, not sole control by Flamingo. Joint control under the EU Merger Regulation requires each parent to hold at least veto rights over strategic decisions such as budget, business plan and senior management. That holds whatever the equity split, for example a 50/50 split or a Flamingo majority with Nazca vetoes.
- **Visquert probably sold or was squeezed.** He is not named as a notifying party. His roughly 6.3% stake was most likely bought by Flamingo, or else he stays as a non-controlling minority. Neither has been reported.
- **"Purchase of shares" points to a secondary sale.** Flamingo likely bought existing shares, possibly with some new money as well. This matches Nazca's January 2025 stated preference for a partial sale plus or minus a capital increase.
- **Likely exit mechanics.** A typical PE joint venture with an industrial partner would include drag/tag rights, a lock-up and a path for Flamingo to take full control later, through a call option or a put for Nazca. This fits Fund IV's end-of-life timing (section 4) and the CEO's 2022 view that Herbex would end up in a larger group. None of these terms has been reported, so this is inference only.
- **Fund-level hold possibility.** The co-control deal may have been structured to give Nazca IV partial liquidity now. The residual stake could be sold to Flamingo later or moved into a continuation vehicle.

### Gaps
- Flamingo's stake percentage, price and enterprise value were not found. No Spanish press report of the signing (Expansión, El Confidencial, Cinco Días or Capital & Corporate) surfaced.
- No report of Nazca's rollover or reinvestment amount, of put/call options, or of drag/tag clauses.
- Closing date not found. Clearance was 14 Aug 2026, so closing was presumably after that.
- The sell-side advisors for 2025–26 are unconfirmed beyond Banco Sabadell's 2025 mandate. Bidders other than Flamingo were never named.
- No CNMC filing was found. EU jurisdiction suggests Sun Capital's turnover triggered EUMR thresholds, which means no CNMC review was needed.

## 4. Fondo Nazca IV, FCR: size, vintage, term and other portfolio companies (exit pressure)

### Takeaway
Fondo Nazca IV FCR held its first close on 30 Sep 2016 and its final close on 24 May 2017, with €276.5m committed by 20 investors. Its term is 10 years from first close, so 30 Sep 2026, extendable to a maximum of about 12 years. In November 2026 the fund is therefore past its base term and in its extension period. That puts strong pressure on Nazca to exit Herbex and Fund IV's other remaining holdings.

### Inferences
- **Exit pressure is high.** With a base term ending around 30 Sep 2026 and a hard stop around 2028, Nazca had to give Fund IV LPs liquidity. The Flamingo joint-control deal signed and notified in July 2026 lines up almost exactly with the base-term expiry. It is very likely a step toward full exit within the extension window, rather than a long-term partnership by Nazca.
- **Herbex is likely an underperformer for Fund IV.** The restructuring, the falling hectares and sales, and the €60m+ value cited in 2025 against €47–60m+ revenue since 2018 all point the same way. That would make Nazca sensitive to price and more likely to accept a staged exit, such as co-control with an earn-out-style put/call, rather than a full sale at a low valuation.
- **Herbex is Fund IV's main agri-food position today.** FoodBox (restaurants) is food-adjacent, and Juan Luna belonged to Fund III.

### Gaps

- Whether Nazca has already triggered the extension was not found.
- Fund IV's DPI/TVPI and remaining NAV were not found.
- The current status of Phibo, Terratest and FoodBox in Fund IV is unknown.

## 6. Nazca's exit track record and behaviour as a minority or co-control shareholder

### Takeaway
Nazca (founded 2001, Madrid, about €500m AUM across seven vehicles, 19 portfolio companies as of Jan 2026) is a control-oriented Spanish lower mid-market buyer. It usually exits by trade sale to an industrial buyer (McBath, Juan Luna, IDP, Teltronic) or by secondary buyout (Cinelux to GED). Exits slowed in 2025, with Filmin unsold and Herbex unsold until the 2026 Flamingo deal. I found no documented case of Nazca sitting long-term as a co-control or minority partner. That supports reading the Herbex joint control as transitional.

### Inferences
- **Herbex is far beyond Nazca's normal hold.** By November 2026 Nazca has held Herbex for about eight years, well past its stated 3–6 years. Together with Fund IV's term, this makes Nazca a motivated seller.
- **What Nazca's retained power likely is.** It most likely consists of contractual veto rights (budget, business plan, M&A, financing, management appointments), board or administrator seats, and exit rights. Those exit rights would be a tag-along, a drag-along after a lock-up, and possibly a put to Flamingo or a joint-sale right. Nazca's leverage comes mainly from these vetoes and from timing its own exit, not from running the business day to day.
- **Who now drives operations and strategy.** Flamingo is an industrial produce and floriculture operator with UK retail channels, so it is the natural operational lead. Nazca's attention will be on value and timing of its exit.
- **Precedent for this kind of sequenced exit.** Nazca's track record of trade sales to industrial buyers (Costa, Engineered Stone, Bureau Veritas, Amper) suggests Flamingo/Sun Capital is the most likely eventual buyer of Nazca's remaining Herbex stake.
