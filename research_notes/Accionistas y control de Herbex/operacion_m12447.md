## 1. EC case M.12447: prior notification, parties, control acquired, decision

### Takeaway
On 23 July 2026 the Commission received notice of a deal in which Flamingo Produce Investments Ltd (UK, Flamingo Group, ultimately controlled by Sun Capital, US) and Fondo Nazca IV (Spanish PE) acquire **joint control of the whole of Herbex** (Herbex Iberia and subsidiaries) by share purchase. Before the deal, Nazca IV had sole control. The notice flagged the case as a candidate for the simplified procedure, and the Commission cleared it under that procedure on **17 Aug 2026**.

### Inferences
- The legal bases cited are Art. 3(1)(b) plus Art. 3(4) EUMR. That combination is the standard formula when an existing company moves from sole control to joint control and becomes a full-function JV. It confirms the Commission treated Herbex as a **full-function joint venture** under the joint control of Flamingo and Nazca IV, with Nazca keeping its stake instead of exiting.
- "Negligible activity in the EEA" for a Spain-based herb grower most likely reflects the quantitative safe harbour in point 5(a) of the 2023 Simplified Procedure Notice: JV EEA turnover and EEA assets each below EUR 100m. That fits Herbex's size (about €47m revenue in 2017-18; see section 3). "No horizontal or vertical relationships" corresponds to point 5(c). The exact category ticked was not visible in what I read.
- A Spanish search summary described the deal as Flamingo taking "el control de la totalidad de Herbex" with Nazca as "fondo vendedor". This conflicts with the notice's explicit "joint control" wording and is probably an AI or paraphrase error. Rely on "joint control".

- The decision's C(2026) reference number and its OJ publication as an "authorisation" notice were not found. A non-confidential decision is normally published later on the case page (competition-cases.ec.europa.eu, blocked).

## 2. Other approvals: Morocco, Spain (CNMC), FDI screening

### Takeaway

Because the deal crossed the EUMR thresholds and went to Brussels, the one-stop-shop rule excludes the CNMC. ### Cited Findings

Contract signed 19 Jul 2022, notification logged 3 Aug 2022 — [Conseil de la concurrence communiqué (PDF)](https://conseil-concurrence.ma/cc/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Communique-francais-OCE-Africulture-Aromafresh.pdf); [Decision 100](https://conseil-concurrence.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/decision-100.pdf)

- Moroccan joint-control doctrine: under the Conseil's guidelines, joint control is assessed on all legal and factual circumstances. The Conseil does publish communiqués on joint-control deals (e.g., Partners Group/Eurazeo over Aroma Zone; FM6I over MARBIO) — [Lignes directrices (PDF)](https://conseil-concurrence.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/LignesDirectrices_ConcentrationsEconomiques_Vers1-de-DEC-2023.pdf); [Aroma Zone communiqué](https://conseil-concurrence.ma/communique-du-conseil-de-la-concurrence-relatif-au-projet-de-concentration-economique-concernant-la-prise-par-des-fonds-conseilles-et-ou-geres-par-la-societe-partners-group-ag-et-ses-fi/)

### Inferences
- **CNMC**: an EU-dimension concentration is reviewed only by the Commission (Art. 21(3) EUMR). No CNMC filing would be expected unless there was an Art. 9 referral, and I found none. (Inference from the legal framework; not checked on cnmc.es.)
- **Morocco**: Moroccan merger control applies when worldwide or Moroccan turnover thresholds are met, and it can apply to foreign-to-foreign deals. Flamingo/Sun Capital's worldwide turnover could trigger it if the target or the parties have Moroccan turnover. If Herbex no longer has a Moroccan subsidiary after 2022, a filing may still have been needed on worldwide-turnover grounds. Status unknown.
- **Spanish FDI screening** (Art. 7 bis Ley 19/2003; RD 571/2023): Flamingo is a non-EU/EFTA investor (UK, ultimately US) acquiring at least 10% or control of a Spanish company. Agri-food / food-security supply is a listed strategic sector, so prior authorisation may have been required if Herbex's investment or turnover exceeded the de minimis thresholds. This is plausible, but I found no source confirming a filing or authorisation.

## 4. Legal meaning of joint control (EUMR) and implications for Flamingo vs Nazca

### Takeaway
Under the Commission's Consolidated Jurisdictional Notice (2008/C 95/01), joint control means each of Flamingo and Nazca IV can **block strategic decisions** of Herbex. Typically this is a veto over budget, business plan, major investments or senior management appointments, so the two must agree on Herbex's strategic commercial behaviour. Neither can impose decisions alone. Public detail on the actual Herbex shareholders' agreement is **nil**. Everything beyond the legal standard is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Standard: in a joint venture where both shareholders have veto rights over strategic matters such as the business plan, budget or senior management, they must reach consensus on the commercial policy and so jointly control it. Pure minority protections (vetoes over changes to the articles, capital increases or decreases, liquidation) do not confer control — [Chambers Merger Control 2025 (EU chapter)](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/merger-control-2025/192/); [Consolidated Jurisdictional Notice text (CELEX 52008XC0416(08))](https://prawo.uni.wroc.pl/sites/default/files/students-resources/CELEX_52008XC0416%2808%29_EN_TXT.pdf); [vLex copy](https://vlex.es/vid/consolidated-jurisdictional-notice-446986)
- A single veto right over a strategic matter (e.g., the budget or business plan) can suffice to give joint control, depending on its content and importance to the business — [Chambers Merger Control 2025](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/merger-control-2025/471)
- A substantive veto over the business plan and budget gives a minority shareholder decisive influence. This matters both for consolidation (accounting) and for negotiating JV terms — [Freshfields blog](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/transactions/to-consolidate-or-not-and-the-impact-on-negotiations-in-a-joint-venture-context-102i8jg)
- The Moroccan Conseil applies the same "all legal and factual circumstances" test for joint control — [Lignes directrices (PDF)](https://conseil-concurrence.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Lignes-directrices-Francais-14-02-2024.pdf)

### Inferences (based on the CJN, general knowledge; not Herbex-specific evidence)
- **CJN framework** (from my knowledge of the Notice, paras. ~62-82; specific paragraph numbers not checked because eur-lex was blocked): joint control exists where two or more undertakings can exercise decisive influence, usually through the possibility of blocking strategic decisions. It can arise from (a) equality of voting rights or board seats (50/50), (b) veto rights over budget, business plan, major investments or senior management appointment, or (c) joint exercise of voting rights through a pooling agreement or strong common interests. The veto must cover strategic decisions on the JV's business policy and need not be exercised in practice; the possibility is enough. Joint control is also "full-function" (Art. 3(4)) when the JV performs on a lasting basis all the functions of an autonomous economic entity. The Commission treated Herbex that way.
- **What the change means for each shareholder:**
  - *Nazca IV* moves from sole control (≈94%) to joint control. It loses the ability to set Herbex's strategy alone but keeps a blocking veto on strategic decisions. That is consistent with Nazca staying invested while bringing in an industrial partner.
  - *Flamingo* (Sun Capital) gains a strategic veto, and probably board seats, without needing a majority. This is consistent with either a minority entry (with vetoes) or a majority entry in which Nazca keeps vetoes. **Who holds the majority is unknown.** A "joint control" finding does not tell us the equity split.
  - Neither shareholder can unilaterally approve Herbex's budget or business plan, major capex or M&A (e.g., further bolt-ons like Magnani), or appoint or remove the CEO and senior management. Deadlock is possible by design.
  - Any later move by one party to sole control (e.g., Flamingo buying out Nazca) would be a **new concentration** (joint → sole control) needing fresh merger-control analysis, possibly another EUMR or national filing.
- **Typical PE / industrial co-control SHA terms** (market practice, not Herbex-specific): reserved matters lists (budget, business plan, capex above a threshold, debt incurrence, M&A, senior hires, dividend policy, related-party transactions); board composition mirroring the vetoes; deadlock resolution (escalation to principals, mediation, then a Russian roulette / Texas shoot-out or put/call); lock-up period, ROFO/ROFR and tag-along / drag-along. A typical path to full control for the industrial partner is a call option over the PE stake (and a put for the PE fund) exercisable after a set period or on an exit window, often priced on an EBITDA multiple. Fondo Nazca IV (2016 vintage per Unquote) is late in its life, which makes an exit mechanism (drag, put, IPO or trade-sale right) likely. Inference — [Unquote, Fondo Nazca IV vintage](https://www.unquote.com/southern-europe/official-record/3012145/nazca-capital-backs-herbex)
- Herbex's position as founder-led (Visquert) and any management equity or retention arrangements are unknown.
