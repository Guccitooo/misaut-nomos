Research date: 10 November 2026. Method: web search only. ## Q0. Context: the Herbex transaction (M.12447)

### Takeaway
The European Commission received notice on 23 July 2026 that Flamingo and Nazca (Fondo Nazca IV) would take joint control of all of Herbex through a share purchase. It was handled under simplified procedure and cleared. Sun therefore holds its influence over Herbex through Flamingo's joint-control rights, not through sole control.

### Cited Findings
- [OJ notice, 2026] Case M.12447 FLAMINGO / FONDO NAZCA IV / HERBEX. Notified to the Commission on 23 July 2026. Flamingo and Nazca acquire joint control of the whole of Herbex by purchase of shares. Flagged as a candidate for simplified procedure. Flamingo is described as growing, processing and supplying fresh cut flowers, house and garden plants and premium produce. Nazca is described as a mid-market private equity fund — [EUR-Lex OJ C/2026/04235](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC_202604235)
- [2026] The Commission cleared the deal under simplified review. Its reasons: the JV has negligible EEA activities, and the parties are not active in the same or vertically linked markets — [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2276093/eu-clears-uk-spanish-control-of-fresh-herbs-venture-under-simplified-review)
- [2018 onward] Nazca took a majority stake in Herbex (El Ejido, Almería) in 2018. A later report gave Nazca's holding as 93.69% — [Hortidaily 2018](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9032044/spain-nazca-private-equity-becomes-majority-shareholder-of-aromatic-herbs-company/); [Nazca press release](https://www.nazca.es/en/noticias/herbex-acelera-su-expansion-internacional-e-incorpora-a-nazca-a-su-accionariado/). Trade press later ran "If Nazca leaves, Herbex might be part of large agri-food group" — [Hortidaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9400427/if-nazca-leaves-herbex-might-be-part-of-large-agri-food-group/) — and "Private equity funds are interested in Herbex" — [Hortidaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9697609/private-equity-funds-are-interested-in-herbex/)

### Inferences
- With joint control, Flamingo (and so Sun) has decisive influence, i.e. veto rights over strategic matters such as the budget, business plan and senior management. It does not unilaterally run Herbex. Nazca IV remains a co-controller with its own fund-life exit clock.
- The structure looks like a staged path to full ownership by Flamingo, a common buy-and-build pattern: a JV or minority stake with call/put options that lets the incumbent fund exit later. This is an inference. The shareholders' agreement terms are not public.

### Gaps
- Stake percentages, shareholders' agreement terms (options, drag/tag rights, board seats) and the Commission decision date were not found. These are covered by the Herbex-specific research.

## Q1. When and how Sun acquired Flamingo; fund; holding vehicles; board

### Takeaway
Sun Capital's European arm, Sun European Partners, bought the business from James Finlay (Finlays) in October/November 2015, reportedly for about £155m. It had been called Finlays Horticulture and took back the Flamingo Horticulture name in January 2016. Sun then added to it through a series of acquisitions: Butters Group (2017), Afriflora of Ethiopia from KKR (Dec 2017, financed by a ~€300–310m leveraged loan), and Bigot Group of France (Dec 2021). The UK holding chain runs Zara UK Holdco Ltd (2017) → Zara UK Topco Ltd (2015) → Flamingo entities. The specific Sun fund is not confirmed in any source.

### Inferences
- From the timing (Oct 2015) and the Topco incorporation (July 2015), the acquisition was very likely made from Sun Capital Partners VI (2013/14 vintage). This is the main inference to verify. If correct, Flamingo sits in a fund that would be 12–13 years old by late 2026, well past a normal 10-year term. That would imply fund extensions and strong LP pressure for liquidity (see Q3/Q5).
- "Zara" is the deal codename. Holdco being inserted in Nov 2017 matches the Afriflora acquisition and the €310m refinancing in Dec 2017/Jan 2018. It was probably created as the new top borrower or obligor entity for the leveraged loan.
- "Sun European Partners" (London LLP, Sun's European adviser) runs the asset day to day. The ownership capital comes from Sun Capital flagship funds. Sources use the two names interchangeably.

## Q2. Sun's governance model; dividend recaps and fees from Flamingo

### Takeaway
Sun presents itself as an operationally intensive buy-and-build investor. Its funds take control positions, and an in-house operating team works with management. For Flamingo, the documented pattern is leverage-financed add-ons (the €310m loan for Afriflora). No public source confirms a dividend recap or a fee figure taken out of Flamingo.

### Cited Findings

Its site lists 13 portfolio companies, about €1bn raised, and €2.5bn portfolio revenue — [Sun European Partners website](https://suneuropeanpartners.com/); [Sun European news](https://suneuropeanpartners.com/news)

## Q3. Holding period; exit processes, sale rumours, banker mandates

### Takeaway
At November 2026, Sun has held Flamingo for about 11 years (since Oct/Nov 2015), which is very long for PE. Sun ran a public sale process in late 2021, with JP Morgan hired, a reported valuation of about £750–850m (Telegraph) or more than €1bn (Bloomberg), and both PE and strategic bidders expected. No sale followed. No reliable 2024–2026 report of a new process was found. In February 2026 Sun began using single-asset continuation vehicles (CVs), which is the most likely precedent for a Flamingo liquidity event.

### Cited Findings
- [30 Sep 2021] Sun European was weighing a sale of Flamingo (customers include Tesco and Aldi), working with advisers. Valuation possibly above €1bn; PE and strategic interest expected — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-09-30/sun-capital-said-to-weigh-sale-of-tesco-flower-supplier-flamingo); [PitchBook newsletter](https://pitchbook.com/newsletter/sun-capital-eyes-sale-of-flamingo-horticulture-at-12b-value)
- [28 Nov 2021] Sun European hired JP Morgan for a sale. The headline said £750m and the subheading said £850m — [Telegraph (mirror)](https://dleith.co.uk/www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2021/11/28/tesco-flower-supplier-flamingo-hires-bankers-750m-sale/)
- [Dec 2021] While the process was live, Flamingo signed the Bigot Group add-on — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/news/2021/12/dla-piper-advises-flamingo)
- [2026] Sun still lists Flamingo as a current portfolio company seeking add-ons — [Sun Capital portfolio](https://suncappart.com/portfolio/)
- [10 Feb 2026] Sun completed its first single-asset CV, for Fletchers Solicitors (UK), at about £240m, oversubscribed. Fund VII LPs could take liquidity or roll over. The GP rolled its whole position and added $10m through its Sun Capital Star Fund secondaries vehicle. Advisers were Houlihan Lokey and Kirkland — [Houlihan Lokey](https://hl.com/about-us/transactions/sun-capital-partners-sun-european-partners-fletchers-solicitors/); [Kirkland, Mar 2026](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2026/03/kirkland-advises-sun-capital-partners-on-single-asset-continuation-vehicle-for-fletchers-solicitors); [Sun press release](https://suncappart.com/2026/02/11/sun-capital-affiliate-completes-single-asset-continuation-vehicle-of-affiliated-portfolio-company-fletchers-solicitors/)
- [2026] A second CV followed: Fund VIII agreed to sell Anderson Global into a Sun-managed single-asset CV. In Feb 2026 it was expected to close in Q2/Q3. A Sept 2026 newsletter reports it closed — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/sun-capital-completes-first-single-asset-continuation-vehicle-for-fletchers-solicitors/); [The Secondary Brief, 29 Sep 2026](https://buttondown.com/thesecondarybrief/archive/2026-09-29/)
- [Oct 2025] Sun exited Cotton Holdings (US) to Sullivan Brothers — [Sun press release](https://suncappart.com/2025/10/28/sun-capital-exits-cotton-holdings/)

### Inferences
- The 2021 auction apparently failed, likely on valuation (a ~€1bn ask against about £64m EBITDA in 2023 implies roughly 13–14x). Flamingo then entered a "hold-and-build" phase. Buying into Herbex in 2026 fits a strategy of adding higher-growth, higher-margin categories (fresh herbs, Spain/Mediterranean sourcing) to support a re-run exit equity story.
- With 11 years held, a probable Fund VI owner and two 2026 CVs, the likeliest Flamingo exits within 12–24 months are: (a) a single-asset CV, (b) a sale to a strategic or another sponsor, or (c) a partial sale. Herbex's JV structure should be read with this in mind: a change of control at Flamingo would pass Flamingo's Herbex rights to a new owner unless change-of-control clauses say otherwise.
- No public 2024–2026 banker mandate for Flamingo was found. Its absence from press does not rule out a quiet process.

## Q4. Sun's track record in European food/agri/consumer; failures and controversies

### Takeaway
In European food, Sun's record is mainly long holds of UK chilled/fresh suppliers sold to strategic buyers. Freshpak was held 2016–2025 and sold to PAI-backed Compleat Food Group. The firm-level reputation risk comes from its US retail and consumer bankruptcies (Shopko, Marsh, Friendly's, Scott Brass pension case). These involved sale-leasebacks, dividends and pension underfunding.

### Cited Findings
- [14 Jul 2025] Sun European sold Freshpak Chilled Foods (Barnsley; egg products, deli fillers, food-to-go; about £93m revenue in 2024; about 500 staff) to The Compleat Food Group (PAI-backed) for an undisclosed sum. DC Advisory advised. Sun European had owned it since 2016, about 9 years — [DC Advisory](https://www.dcadvisory.com/news-deals-insights/deal-announcements/dc-advisory-advises-sun-european-partners-on-the-sale-of-freshpak-to-the-compleat-food-group/); [Just Food](https://just-food.com/news/the-compleat-food-group-snaps-up-freshpak/); [PE Insights](https://pe-insights.com/?p=344470)
- [Mar 2026] Sun European invested in B&H Worldwide (aviation logistics) "adding to its growing buy and build portfolio" — [Yahoo Finance / press release](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/sun-european-affiliate-completes-investment-080000796.html)
- Scott Brass: Sun funds bought it in 2006–07 and it went bankrupt in 2008. The Teamsters fund sought about $4.5m in withdrawal liability. The District Court found the Sun funds liable as a partnership-in-fact. The First Circuit reversed in Nov 2019 — [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/sun-capital-update-court-of-appeals-reverses-district-courts-finding); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2019/11/sun-capital-prevails-in-long-running-erisa-liability-dispute-but-pension-risks); [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/alerts/Comp-and-Benefits-Brief/all/sun-capital-wins-but-all-private-equity-funds-may-not)
- Marsh Supermarkets (bought 2006): pension underfunded by about $76m at bankruptcy, and the PBGC took over the plan. Sun said its investment "kept the company alive". Shopko (2005 LBO; Chapter 11 in 2019): real estate sold for $815m in a sale-leaseback — [AFR case study (advocacy)](https://ourfinancialsecurity.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Sun-Capital-Case-Study.pdf); [Wolf Street, Jan 2019](https://wolfstreet.com/2019/01/17/another-retail-chain-owned-stripped-bare-by-sun-capital-goes-bankrupt/)
- Fletchers CV: LTM EBITDA grew from £8.0m to £37.9m (to 30 Sep 2025) under Sun, an example of a successful UK buy-and-build — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/sun-capital-completes-first-single-asset-continuation-vehicle-for-fletchers-solicitors/)

### Inferences
- Sun's European food playbook (Freshpak, Flamingo) is a long hold, add-ons, and then a sale to a larger strategic or sponsor-backed consolidator. The US controversies are mostly 2005–2010 retail LBOs. They are relevant as evidence of willingness to extract value (sale-leasebacks, dividends) and to structure around liabilities, but are less predictive for an agri-produce JV.
- Herbex-specific risks to watch: (1) Herbex being pulled into Flamingo's debt group as guarantor or security provider if Flamingo moves to full control; (2) Herbex being packaged as part of a Flamingo exit; (3) intra-group supply and pricing tilted toward Flamingo's UK retail contracts.

### Gaps
- No complete list of Sun European food/consumer exits and failures (e.g. older UK retail holdings) was compiled. No reliable aggregate returns data was found.

## Q5. Sun Capital current fund status, AUM and exit-timing pressure

### Takeaway
Sun's flagship fundraising has shrunk across vintages: Fund VI about $2.1bn (2014, below its $3bn target), Fund VII $2.3bn (2019), and Fund VIII about $1.4bn (2022 vintage, against a $2.5bn target). Reported firm AUM is about $5.6bn (Mar 2026; low-quality source). No Fund IX fundraising was confirmed. Older funds that are probably past term, combined with Sun's 2026 move into CVs, point to strong pressure to return capital.

### Cited Findings
- Fund VI: about $2.1bn final close (June 2014), against a $3bn target, after about 18 months of fundraising — [PE Hub](https://www.pehub.com/sun-capital-fund-vi-closes-at-2-1-bln-below-3-bln-target-2/); [PitchBook fund profile](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/fund/13053-61F); [PEI institution profile](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/institution-profiles/sun-capital-partners.html)

A March 2023 filing shows about $1.41bn sold — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/fund/20322-91F); [aum13f](https://aum13f.com/fund/sun-capital-partners-viii-a-lp); [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/top-1000-global-private-equity-firms/sun-capital-partners/)

### Inferences
- If Flamingo sits in Fund VI (2013/14), that fund is past its standard 10-year life by 2026 and probably on extensions. Fund VI LPs (and possibly Fund VII) will want liquidity. A Flamingo exit or CV in 2026–2028 is therefore likely, and the Herbex add-on may be part of preparing the equity story.
- Shrinking fund sizes point to weaker LP demand. Strong DPI from older assets helps Sun raise its next fund, which adds to exit pressure. Expect Sun to favour a full exit or a CV over a further long hold.

### Gaps
- The fund holding Flamingo is unconfirmed. Fund VI remaining NAV and term extensions are unknown, as is any Fund IX launch (2025–2026). Primary Sun press releases on fundraising could not be fetched (domain blocked).
